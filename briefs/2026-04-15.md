## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

- **Anthropic confirms "Claude Mythos" exists — its most capable model ever — and it will not be publicly released.** Only 50 organizations get access under a program called Project Glasswing. This is a consequential signal: we've entered the era of tiered frontier access. If you're building enterprise AI and you're not in that cohort, you're not playing at the ceiling. The capability gap between public and private models just became a real business risk.

- **Claude Opus 4.7 reportedly launching this week, alongside a new AI design tool for websites/presentations.** Anthropic is maintaining an aggressive cadence. For builders on the Claude API — expect capability lifts and check for any breaking changes. The design tool is a potential Canva/Figma disruptor worth watching.

- **OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google form a defensive intelligence pact via Frontier Model Forum to stop Chinese adversarial distillation.** The mechanism: sharing signals about model theft attempts to harden against labs training on their outputs without authorization. Rare industry cooperation — and a sign that model IP is now treated as geopolitical infrastructure, not just competitive IP.

- **Meta launches "Muse Spark" — first model from Meta Superintelligence Labs under Alexandr Wang.** Hybrid open-source/proprietary approach, unlike the fully open LLaMA lineage. Meta's learned from LLaMA's free-rider problem. The Muse series will be the one to benchmark against GPT-5.4 and Gemini 3.1 — early results pending.

- **MCP crosses 97M monthly SDK downloads and 10,000+ active public servers; Lucidworks ships enterprise MCP server promising 10x faster agent integration timelines.** MCP is now load-bearing infrastructure. The enterprise integration complexity barrier — previously the primary obstacle to scaling AI agents — is collapsing. If you're building agentic platforms without standardizing on MCP, you're accumulating serious technical debt.

- **NVIDIA debuts Nemotron 3 open model family, built explicitly for agentic workflows.** Nano variant available now at low inference cost; optimized for debugging, summarization, and AI assistant tasks. This is NVIDIA's bid to own the agentic model layer, not just the hardware. Open model + owned silicon = powerful distribution flywheel.

- **AMD MI450 ships on TSMC 2nm — the first major GPU on that node, potentially beating NVIDIA's Rubin (slated for 3nm in H2 2026).** AMD holds ~13% of the AI accelerator market but analysts project 20–25% by late 2026, driven by the Meta $60B deal and OpenAI infrastructure commitments. The 2nm advantage matters for the next generation of inference efficiency — enterprise hardware buyers should be watching this closely.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** AMD MI450 on TSMC 2nm is flying under the radar. If AMD ships on 2nm ahead of NVIDIA's Rubin (3nm, H2 2026), the efficiency-per-watt math could meaningfully shift enterprise GPU procurement decisions in Q3/Q4. Anyone speccing out inference infrastructure for late 2026 should be stress-testing AMD into their vendor mix now — not after the benchmark reviews drop.

**One-Liner:** The AI stack is stratifying — frontier models are being hoarded behind access programs, protocols are becoming enterprise-critical infrastructure, and the hardware war just entered its most competitive node yet.
