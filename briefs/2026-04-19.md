## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

🚢 **Iran closes Strait of Hormuz again** — Citing "repeated breaches of trust" in ceasefire talks, Iran is shutting down the strait for the second time in the ongoing Middle East conflict. The Strait handles ~20% of global oil; even a temporary closure sends energy markets into a tailspin and adds another inflation vector to an already strained macro picture.

🪖 **Ukraine war shows no sign of slowing** — Day 1,516: 153 combat engagements, 9,360 kamikaze drones deployed in 24 hours, plus a gunman killed 6 in a Kyiv mass shooting unrelated to the frontlines. The operational tempo remains brutal, and without a political offramp, commodity markets and European defense budgets stay under pressure indefinitely.

💊 **Trump tariffs go nuclear on pharma** — New Section 232 tariffs impose up to 100% on patented drug imports; Fed economists say tariffs "explain entirely" the excess core goods inflation since January 2025. Households face ~$2,100 in average purchasing power loss. Powell is stuck on hold; markets now price Fed funds at 3.0% by year-end, a full cut below pre-"Liberation Day" expectations.

📊 **IMF April WEO: global economy "in the shadow of war"** — The Fund flags rising commodity prices, firming inflation expectations, and tightening financial conditions from the Middle East conflict. Classic stagflation territory, and the IMF isn't being subtle about it.

💰 **OpenAI raises another $10B; Q1 global venture hits $300B record** — OpenAI's latest $10B tops up its February megaround. Q1 2026 shattered all prior global VC records at $300B. Obesity drug IPO Kailera surged 63% on debut. Capital is still flowing enthusiastically despite macro headwinds — the AI premium on capital allocation remains fully intact.

🌊 **AMOC could weaken 42–58% by 2100 — worse than previously thought** — A Science Advances study combining observational data with models landed on projections significantly worse than prior estimates. A weaker Atlantic overturning circulation means a colder Europe, stronger Atlantic hurricanes, and faster sea level rise on the U.S. East Coast. The kind of slow-moving catastrophe that gets ignored until it doesn't.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

🔐 **Anthropic's Claude Mythos: 83% on CyberGym, gated to 50 orgs only** — The most capable model Anthropic has ever built won't be publicly released. "Project Glasswing" gives gated access to just 50 organizations; it scored 83.1% on the CyberGym autonomous vulnerability discovery benchmark vs. 66.6% for its predecessor. The gap between what frontier labs have internally and what's commercially available is widening fast — a critical calibration signal for anyone building on public APIs.

💻 **GPT-5.4 ships with native computer-use; OpenAI also drops a dedicated cybersecurity model** — GPT-5.4 is OpenAI's first general-purpose model with native, state-of-the-art computer-use baked in — agents can now operate computers without wrappers or scaffolding. GPT-5.4-Cyber is purpose-built for digital defenders. **Directly relevant to agentic platform builders**: computer-use as a first-class native capability changes the tool integration calculus considerably.

🏢 **EY deploys multi-agent AI to 130,000 auditors across 150 countries** — Built on Microsoft Azure/Foundry/Fabric and embedded in EY Canvas. One of the largest single enterprise agentic deployments ever announced — 160,000 audits, 130,000 professionals. The audit industry is now the unexpected bellwether for enterprise agentic scale.

📦 **TSMC Q1 revenue +35% YoY to $35.7B; advanced packaging is the next AI bottleneck** — NVIDIA has reserved the majority of TSMC's CoWoS advanced packaging capacity; TSMC is ramping four new packaging facilities (two in Taiwan, two in Arizona). ASML confirmed AI spending shows no signs of slowing. **The constraint is shifting from chip fabrication to packaging** — a supply chain nuance with direct implications for AI infrastructure planning timelines.

🤖 **NVIDIA launches open agent development platform** — Positioned as "the next industrial revolution in knowledge work." NVIDIA's move to own the agentic software stack — not just the silicon — is the long-term strategic play to watch. Details still emerging.

📊 **94% of enterprises worry about agent sprawl — OutSystems research** — Agentic AI has gone mainstream in the enterprise, but governance and sprawl are the dominant concern. The tooling gap between *deploying* agents and *managing* them is wide open. **This is the biggest white space in enterprise AI right now** and the clearest signal for where platform investment should go.

🔬 **Nature study: human scientists still outperform best AI on complex research tasks** — Despite frontier benchmark gains, AI agents underperform humans on genuinely complex, novel scientific tasks. Important calibration for anyone overselling AI research autonomy to customers or investors. The benchmarks are not the product.

## Sleeper Story

**Broadcom's multi-year custom chip deal with Meta.** Broadcom will design and supply custom AI chips for Meta's training and inference at scale. As custom silicon becomes the default for hyperscalers, NVIDIA's moat increasingly depends on software lock-in (CUDA) rather than hardware exclusivity. This deal quietly erodes that narrative — and it's getting almost no coverage relative to its strategic significance. Watch for this to become a much bigger story in 30–60 days as Meta's custom deployments scale and the NVIDIA dependency narrative gets stress-tested.

## One-Liner

*The world is running hot on every front — a closing strait, tariff-fueled stagflation, and AI capabilities racing ahead of governance — and nobody has a hand on the brake.*
