# Morning Download — May 25, 2026

## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

- 🏦 **Kevin Warsh sworn in as Fed Chair — narrowest confirmation in history** — Trump installed his pick at the Fed on May 22, confirmed 54-45, replacing Powell. Warsh promised a "reform-oriented" Fed that escapes "static frameworks." Translation: expect a more politically attentive central bank. Markets want cuts; Warsh is hawkish. That tension will define monetary policy for the next four years.

- 🤝 **US-China trade truce: readout gaps signal fragility** — The May 12 deal cut tariffs from 145%→30%, the biggest thaw since the trade war began. But analysts spotted inconsistencies between US and Chinese readouts on chips, rare earths, and agriculture. China suspended antitrust probes of Nvidia/Qualcomm/Intel pending negotiations. This is progress — but it's conditional, not durable.

- 🇷🇺 **Russia fires nuclear-capable Oreshnik missile at Kyiv: 2 dead, 77 injured** — The Oreshnik hypersonic ballistic missile can carry nukes. Using it on a civilian target is a deliberate escalatory signal, not just a tactical strike. Ukraine's air defenses had no answer for it. The deterrence calculus in this war just changed.

- 🦠 **Ebola spreading into Uganda as 10-country outbreak accelerates** — Infections have crossed from eastern Congo into Uganda, with the WHO racing to contain spread into neighboring countries. If containment fails in the next 30 days, this becomes a global logistics and health system story — and a supply chain concern for companies operating across East Africa.

- 📈 **S&P 500 up 27% YoY, markets hit records as Warsh era begins** — US markets closed Monday for Memorial Day. The S&P 500 ran its 8-week winning streak — longest since December 2023 — and Q1 blended net profit margins hit 13.4%, an all-time record per FactSet. Futures surged on Iran/Strait of Hormuz optimism. The economy looks spectacular on the surface; a hawkish new Fed chair and political trade risk are the understory.

- 🚀 **WILDCARD: China sends astronaut on a 1-year space mission aboard Shenzhou 23** — One crew member will stay aboard China's space station for a full year to study long-duration spaceflight physiology. That's a Mars preparation mission in everything but name. The US hasn't run a comparable experiment in years. Space is very much a live geopolitical competition.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

- 🤖 **Anthropic and OpenAI both launch enterprise arms within 72 hours** — Both companies announced enterprise deployment divisions and major financial-services partnerships targeting Wall Street's most critical workflows, shipping agent tooling for knowledge work and trading operations simultaneously. The enterprise AI land-grab has moved from pilots to full competitive mode — financial services is the first major institutional battleground.

- 🚫 **Trump kills AI pre-release vetting executive order, citing competitive risk** — The order would have required safety review of advanced AI models before public deployment — a response to security concerns around Anthropic's Claude Mythos. Trump pulled it at the last moment. Short-term win for labs; this leaves a safety governance vacuum that the EU will fill and that China will exploit as a narrative weapon.

- 🧠 **Claude "Mythos" reportedly uncovering decades-old vulnerabilities in banking systems** — Anthropic's advanced model is finding critical bugs in legacy financial infrastructure that had gone undetected for decades. This is dual-use in the clearest sense: tremendous value for defensive security teams, and a significant risk signal for any organization running legacy core banking on COBOL or pre-2000 systems.

- 📡 **MCP has won the AI integration protocol war** — 16 months old and already embedded in every major AI coding assistant: Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, and Codex all support it. JetBrains shipped IDE-native MCP tools with AST and symbol-level indexing. Prismatic released an open-source Claude Code plugin for enterprise integrations. For agentic platform builders: MCP is the integration layer — treat it as infrastructure, not a feature.

- 🖥️ **NVIDIA locks up TSMC's CoWoS packaging capacity, creating a compute bottleneck** — NVIDIA has reserved the majority of TSMC's most advanced packaging capacity; TSMC is outsourcing overflow to ASE and Amkor. TSMC 2nm is on track to ramp H2 2026, but advanced packaging — not chip fabrication — is the binding constraint on AI compute supply this year. Expect this to throttle Blackwell successor deployments.

- 🔍 **Google ships Gemini 3.5 Flash at one-third frontier cost, debuts Gemini Spark agent** — Gemini 3.5 Flash delivers near-frontier performance at dramatically lower price. Gemini Spark is a new cross-app reasoning agent inside the Gemini app. Google is competing on cost and capability simultaneously, which squeezes the mid-tier model market and makes it harder for any single-model startup to carve out pricing power.

- 🛠️ **Anthropic acquires developer-tools startup Stainless** — Stainless builds SDK generation tooling. Anthropic owning more of the developer API layer is a strategic move: whoever makes the Claude API the path of least resistance for developers wins long-term mindshare even if competitors have comparable models. Small acquisition, large signal.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** The US Court of International Trade ruled Trump's Section 122 global tariffs unlawful. If DOJ appeals and loses, it could unwind the legal architecture underpinning the US-China trade truce — right as that truce is the most fragile it has ever been. A ruling this consequential flying this far under the radar is unusual. Watch for an appeal filing within 30 days.

**One-Liner:** The AI industry has graduated from "what can it do?" to "who controls it?" — and today, regulators, enterprises, Wall Street, and Beijing are all fighting over the answer at the same time.
