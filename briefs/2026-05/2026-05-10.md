# Morning Download — May 10, 2026

## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

- 🇺🇸🇨🇳 **Trump-Xi Beijing summit next week amid tariff whiplash — Geneva truce holding by a thread.** A 90-day tariff truce struck in Geneva brought lofty tariffs down, but Trump is now claiming China violated the deal on critical minerals exports. Concept of a standing "Board of Trade" — essentially managed trade with purchase commitments — is floating as the most substantive potential outcome. This is the most consequential bilateral meeting in years; enterprise software companies with China revenue exposure should be watching the managed-trade framing closely.

- 🏦 **Kevin Warsh advances toward Fed chair (Powell's term expires May 15) as rates hold at 3.5–3.75%.** Senate Banking Committee advanced Warsh 13-11 with a full floor vote pending; Powell is staying on the board through Jan 2028 — a nearly unprecedented move. Q1 GDP grew 2.0%, S&P earnings blended growth at +15.1% YoY, but higher oil prices are complicating the inflation picture. Warsh is a known hawk who criticized the Fed's post-COVID inflation response; rate-sensitive tech valuations should price in a shift in tone.

- ⚖️ **US Trade Court rules Trump's 10% global tariff illegal — but effectively only for two importers and Washington State.** The levies stay in place for everyone else while the administration appeals, meaning near-zero operational relief. A ruling that looks decisive but isn't: business can't plan around a law being illegal only for two parties. Watch for the appeals timeline.

- 💊 **Roche acquires PathAI for up to $1.05B; Snap lays off 1,000 citing AI efficiency gains.** Roche's move signals AI diagnostics M&A is in full swing — expect more consolidation in clinical AI. Snap's mass layoff (roughly 25% of planned headcount) is a leading indicator of the structural headcount compression that AI productivity claims have been promising for two years. It's happening faster than expected.

- 🤝 **Intel secures preliminary chip manufacturing deal with Apple — stock up 200%+ YTD, AMD soaring, Nvidia lags.** Wall Street is explicitly calling a "changing of the guard" in AI chips: Intel/AMD are the new momentum trades as Nvidia plateaus at +15% YTD. Intel-Apple deal directly reduces TSMC single-supplier risk for Apple and signals Intel's foundry turnaround is credible. Biggest shift in semiconductor market dynamics since 2022.

- 🌌 **Wildcard: Scientists find the universe's fundamental constants sit in a narrow "sweet spot" for life.** New research shows even tiny deviations in physical constants would make cellular fluid mechanics impossible — blood too thick, water too sticky. No commercial angle, but it's the kind of result that makes you stare at the ceiling for a while.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

- 🔐 **Anthropic launches Project Glasswing — shares Claude Mythos Preview with AWS, Apple, Cisco, Google, JPMorgan, and Microsoft for vulnerability hunting.** This is the company's unreleased frontier model being deployed in a controlled security-research program with the most critical infrastructure operators in the world. Two reads: (1) smart strategic deployment that builds enterprise trust before public launch, and (2) the most capable AI being used to find software vulnerabilities before adversaries do. Directly relevant if you're building on Claude APIs — Mythos Preview's capabilities will eventually roll into production models.

- 🚀 **SpaceX and xAI formally merging — Grok embedded into SpaceX operations for autonomous spacecraft and Mars colony development.** Elon Musk is building a vertical AI-to-infrastructure stack that hyperscalers haven't attempted: AI → rockets → energy → Mars. The Grok 4.x models now have a real-world deployment context that no cloud AI vendor can replicate. Watch for competitive responses from Google/Amazon on physical AI integration.

- 🧬 **Novo Nordisk partners with OpenAI for full-stack AI integration — drug discovery through manufacturing, deployed by end of 2026.** This is not a pilot; it's one of the world's largest pharma companies embedding AI across its entire operating model in under 12 months. The pharmaceutical sector is the fastest-moving enterprise vertical for agentic AI right now.

- 📈 **Multi-agent architectures up 327% in four months; Gartner: 40% of enterprise apps to embed AI agents by end of 2026.** AWS announced a strategic collaboration for agentic AI service delivery on May 6. The inflection from "AI features" to "AI-native applications" is happening this year, not next. If you're building agentic delivery platforms, the market is real and accelerating — governance and cost control are the current blockers cited by enterprise buyers.

- 🏷️ **Meta releases Muse Spark — first model from Meta Superintelligence Labs, competitive on multimodal/agentic tasks at a fraction of Llama 4 compute cost.** Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.7 is live and leading on software engineering tasks. Google released Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite at $0.25/M tokens with 2.5× faster response times. The model cost collapse is ongoing — the differentiation game is now orchestration and tooling, not raw capability.

- 🔧 **MCP Reticle launches — "Wireshark for MCP" gives developers real-time visibility into every message, latency spike, and tool invocation with zero performance overhead.** MCP ecosystem now at 97M installs, 9,400+ registry entries, with OAuth 2.1 and enterprise audit support in the 2026 roadmap. Stripe, Vercel, and Fortune 500 data platforms are early enterprise adopters. If you're running MCP servers in production, this debugging tool is immediately useful.

- ⚡ **Intel-Apple manufacturing deal triggers semiconductor rerating — AMD and Intel now the AI infrastructure momentum trades.** TSMC still dominant (Nvidia reserved most advanced packaging capacity through 2028), but real competition is emerging. The GPU supply constraint through 2028 hasn't changed; what has changed is that Intel is now a credible second-source, which slightly relieves the capacity bottleneck for Apple-adjacent AI workloads.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** Kevin Warsh taking over the Fed chair on May 15 is being treated as a smooth transition, but Warsh has historically been far more hawkish than Powell and has publicly criticized the Fed's post-COVID inflation response. If he signals a rate-hike posture — even rhetorically — at his first press conference, tech valuations and AI infrastructure financing rounds will feel it within days. The market is pricing in continuity; Warsh may not deliver it.

**One-Liner:** AI is eating pharmaceuticals, spacecraft, and chip fabs simultaneously while the world's two largest economies prepare a high-stakes summit and the US central bank hands the keys to a hawk — a week with a lot of slow-burning fuses.
