## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

🛡️ **Cybersecurity writer Nicole Perlroth is calling a run of outages a "vulnpocalypse": Microsoft on November 12, the FAA nationwide ground stop on November 13, major U.S. banks on November 16 and the NSA's classified internal network on November 17** — She calls it "just the opening chapter" and ties part of the trend to AI speeding up vulnerability discovery and exploitation. Officials and skeptics disagree: JPMorgan Chase blamed a software bug in on-premise hardware with no evidence of a cyber cause, and CNN-sourced reporting says the NSA outage followed a software patch. Read it as a fragility story, not a proven attack story; for enterprise buyers, the practical takeaway is that patch and change-management discipline is now a board-level resilience question.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** The "vulnpocalypse" framing. Why it matters: whether or not the outages were attacks, the argument that AI collapses the time to exploit a flaw lands on top of a month of real failures at the FAA, banks, Microsoft and the NSA. If you ship or buy agent platforms, expect security reviewers within weeks to ask how fast you can patch, how agent changes are rolled back and what happens to agent workflows when an upstream dependency goes down.

**One-Liner:** The systems that run everything keep breaking, the apps are starting to hide from the agents, and nobody can agree on whether that's a coincidence.
