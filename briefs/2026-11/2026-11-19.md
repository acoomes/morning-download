## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

📉 **Wall Street split on Wednesday, November 18: the Dow fell about 560 points and the S&P 500 extended its losing streak, while the Nasdaq snapped its skid** — Nvidia sold off after its earnings, which dragged on the S&P and the Dow while the broader Nasdaq held up. I could only see headline-level summaries, so I have left the index closing levels out; check them on a terminal before quoting. The takeaway is that a record quarter from the sector's bellwether did not stop the selling, so the AI trade is now trading on people and policy risk, not on demand. If your customers' budgets assume uninterrupted AI spend, expect more board-level questions this quarter.

Sources: [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-today-nasdaq-p-125812319.html) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-stock-earnings.html)

🏦 **The Fed released minutes of its late-October meeting on Wednesday afternoon, and the preview framed them as somewhat stale** — They predate the hotter-than-expected October CPI report, and Governor Waller had suggested a 50-basis-point hike in December if core CPI came in around 0.3% or higher, a threshold the report then met. Governors Cook and Hammack dissented in October in favor of a 25-basis-point hike. I could not find coverage of what the minutes actually said, so I am not characterizing them. Watch how many non-voters leaned toward tightening; the more there are, the harder it is to argue software multiples have stopped being a rate story.

Sources: [Newsquawk](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/primer-fomc-minutes-due-wednesday-1900gmt1400est)

🎲 **Wildcard: Nvidia's board chair said on the earnings call that Jensen Huang will not return as CEO and gave no timeline for finding a successor** — Chair Harvey Jones said the board accepted Huang's resignation unanimously, "won't expedite the CEO before finding exactly the right person," and would not take questions on the circumstances of the resignation or an ongoing investigation. One outlet reported that Huang has said he would return if invited, which conflicts with the board's stance; I could not reconcile the two. CFO Colette Kress remains interim CEO. The world's most valuable chip supplier is now running without a founder and without a date for a replacement, which is a governance fact enterprise buyers of GPU capacity should note.

Sources: [Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/business/nvidia-board-rules-out-jensen-huangs-return-as-ceo-during-earnings-call) · [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/interim-ceo-colette-kress-and-chairman-harvey-jones-try-to-reassure-nvidia-investors/)

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

🤖 **Microsoft used Ignite 2026 to push Copilot toward an agent-first work platform, with Autopilot, an always-on agent, rolling out in preview** — Coverage describes Autopilot as a long-running agent for recurring work, available through Microsoft's Frontier early-access program, with one outlet saying the preview covers eligible U.S. organizations and requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license for the worker assigned the agent. Microsoft also described Copilot Workspaces, where people and agents share context, and extended Purview to classify and secure data across both people and agents. Details come from secondary coverage, and some outlets blur Build and Ignite content, so confirm against Microsoft's own posts. **For agentic-platform builders:** the incumbent suite vendor is packaging agent identity, data governance and a shared workspace as one bundle, so your differentiation has to sit in domain depth and integration, not in basic agent hosting.

Sources: [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/ignite-2026-microsoft-makes-case-be-your-work-operating-system-intelligence-layer) · [RCP Magazine](https://rcpmag.com/articles/2026/11/17/microsoft-ignite-2026-nadella-makes-the-case.aspx) · [Winbuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-opens-preview-of-autopilot-an-ai-assistant-coworkers-can-message-xcxwbn/)

The sources I found predate pricing, so I have not seen the final price or a first trade and have left them out. **For enterprise AI delivery:** if the listing goes ahead, a public model vendor whose compute suppliers are also its shareholders means quarterly disclosure of the economics behind your contract. This one carries over from earlier coverage; the new fact is that pricing and debut are now hours away, not days.

Investors also focused on the leadership uncertainty described above. The 4% figure is one outlet's arithmetic, not company guidance. **For anyone with GPUs on the critical path:** demand is not the constraint, but the market is now discounting the headline numbers, so expect vendor pricing and allocation conversations to stay firm for now.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** Microsoft's decision to extend Purview data classification to agents as well as people. Why it matters: it treats agents as first-class identities that need data policy, which is where enterprise security reviews are heading. Within 30 days, expect rival suites and agent-platform vendors to announce comparable agent identity and data-policy features, and expect your own customers' security teams to ask how your agents are governed. Treat the Purview detail as single-sourced from Constellation Research's Ignite coverage until Microsoft's own posts confirm it.

**One-Liner:** The AI trade got a record quarter, a leaderless chipmaker and an IPO in the same week, and chose to trade the uncertainty.
