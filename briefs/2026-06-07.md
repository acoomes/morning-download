## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

🗳️ **Peru holds its decisive presidential runoff today — Fujimori vs. Sanchez in a polarized race** — Final polling shows far-right Keiko Fujimori with a narrow lead over leftist Roberto Sanchez; outcome determines whether one of Latin America's largest copper producers veers toward resource nationalism. Mining stocks will respond Monday — this is a direct read on the broader regional left-tilt trend.

🛢️ **Strait of Hormuz enters month four of near-standstill — and somehow the S&P 500 just hit all-time highs** — The Iran war has shut down roughly 20% of global oil supply, QatarEnergy remains under force majeure, and Brent is still well above pre-war levels. The divergence between commodity-market distress and equity calm is the defining macro contradiction of this moment — one of these markets is catastrophically wrong.

📊 **Three straight blowout jobs reports (172K, double consensus) bury rate-cut hopes and put hikes back on the table** — New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh opens his first press conference June 17 with rates at 3.50–3.75% and labor refusing to cool. The June 2026 question is no longer "when do we cut" — it's whether hawks can force a hike before year-end. REITs, long-duration bonds, and high-multiple tech are all in a vice.

🏛️ **Israeli military strike in Lebanon triggers sovereignty crisis — President Aoun calls it a "flagrant violation"** — With Lebanon's government under Joseph Aoun trying to establish post-Hezbollah state authority, an Israeli strike is a direct threat to a fragile stabilization experiment. A second active Levantine flashpoint alongside the Iran war compounds tail risks for energy markets and Gulf insurance underwriting.

💼 **Berkshire Hathaway enters homebuilding with $6.8B acquisition of Taylor Morrison** — Buffett's bet on durable housing demand despite elevated rates is a contrarian macro call; it signals Berkshire's view that rate normalization is ahead even as Fed hawks circle. One of Berkshire's largest moves outside insurance and energy in recent years.

🌍 **Wildcard: Trump proposes his own face on a new $250 bill** — First reported by the Washington Post, the proposal would require congressional approval and would be the first living president on U.S. currency since 1929 reforms. Almost certainly dead on arrival — but the international optics at a moment of dollar-confidence pressure are genuinely awkward.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

💰 **Anthropic raises $65B Series H at $965B valuation — now the most valuable AI startup on earth** — Led by Altimeter, Sequoia, and institutional heavyweights including Amazon ($5B), Blackstone, and GIC, the round values Anthropic above OpenAI for the first time. Run-rate revenue crossed $47B in May. The confidential S-1 is already filed — this is a pre-IPO positioning round, and at $965B, the public offering will be one of the largest in tech history. For builders: Claude's infrastructure investment is going to get significantly heavier.

🏛️ **Trump signs AI executive order: voluntary 30-day government access to frontier models before release** — The June 2 EO asks frontier AI developers to submit new models for federal security review 30 days before public release; a Treasury-led "AI cybersecurity clearinghouse" handles vetting. Deliberately non-mandatory — the order explicitly prohibits mandatory preclearance interpretation — but the precedent is live. If allied governments (EU, UK, Japan) formalize reciprocal arrangements, frontier labs face de facto multi-jurisdictional review regardless of any single law.

🖥️ **NVIDIA's RTX Spark Superchip enters the PC market — Jensen Huang takes on Intel and AMD in laptops** — Unveiled at Computex, the RTX Spark is an Arm-based Blackwell-generation GPU+CPU combined chip on TSMC 3nm, debuting this fall in Dell, HP, Lenovo, and Microsoft Surface laptops. AMD and Intel stocks dropped on the announcement. Enterprise implication: capable on-device inference arriving in every business laptop within 12 months changes the local-vs-cloud inference calculus for anyone building agentic workflows.

📊 **Gartner: Enterprise AI coding agents market hits ~$10B — and "agent-washing" is now the primary buyer hazard** — The newly released Magic Quadrant shows coding agents in "competitive realignment" with frontier providers (OpenAI named a Leader) moving up the stack. The buried lede: 84% of enterprise buyers report encountering agent-washed products — chatbots rebadged as agents — during evaluations. Governance, SDLC integration breadth, and pricing transparency are the real differentiators now.

🔌 **GitHub Copilot's shift to AI Credits (usage-based) is live — and developers are not happy** — One week into the June 1 go-live, developer communities are louder than expected: "you get less but pay the same price" is the emerging consensus. Enterprise customers have a grace period through September 1. The signal for agentic platform builders: agent-heavy workflows will see costs scale non-linearly under metered pricing — model your burn before the grace period ends.

🤖 **BYD confirms entry into humanoid robotics — Chinese EV giant bets its battery and AI stack** — Leveraging its manufacturing depth in batteries, sensors, and software, BYD joins the humanoid robotics race alongside Figure AI, 1X, and Boston Dynamics. This isn't a startup play; it's a supply-chain-backed industrial bet. A credible, vertically-integrated Chinese competitor changes the competitive map and accelerates commoditization of humanoid hardware.

🔮 **Wildcard: Ramp raises $750M at $44B valuation — AI-native finance ops is the new enterprise infrastructure category** — Led by Iconiq, GIC, and Ontario Teachers', the round positions Ramp as the dominant AI-native spend management platform. A $44B valuation for what started as a corporate card company signals that enterprise buyers are paying infrastructure-tier multiples for AI automation of CFO-adjacent workflows. Expect competitors (Brex, Coupa, SAP Concur) to either raise aggressively or consolidate in the next 90 days.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** The Trump AI executive order's "voluntary" 30-day pre-release review framework is being written off as toothless — but the GPAI (EU), Japan's AI governance body, and the UK's DSIT have all signaled interest in coordinating pre-release safety evaluations with Washington. If even two allies formalize reciprocal voluntary review arrangements within the next 30 days, frontier labs face de facto multi-jurisdictional pre-clearance processes regardless of what any single law mandates. Watch for GPAI summit communications in June.

**One-Liner:** An AI startup is closing in on a $1 trillion valuation, the U.S. government is asking to read your models before you ship them, and the stock market is at all-time highs while a war blocks 20% of global oil — the word "unprecedented" has lost all remaining meaning.
