## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

🇺🇦 **Ukraine strikes St. Petersburg oil terminal; Russia pounds Kyiv** — Ukrainian drones torched an oil terminal in St. Petersburg during Putin's annual economic forum while Russian forces launched a massive aerial assault on Kyiv and other cities, killing at least 22 civilians and wounding 138. The St. Petersburg strike was timed to humiliate Putin on his own stage — expect a hardened Western posture heading into summer and fresh pressure on European energy prices.

📊 **Broadcom misses, CrowdStrike guidance disappoints — Dow sheds 532 points** — Broadcom reported Q2 revenue of $22.19B vs. $22.27B expected (its first miss since December 2024) and fell ~15%; CrowdStrike dropped 9% on soft Q2 guidance despite record Q1 net new ARR of $256M (+32% YoY). For enterprise software buyers, back-to-back misses from a chip bellwether and a cybersecurity leader are a yellow flag for Q3 budget conversations.

🏦 **Fed holds steady; June 16-17 FOMC consensus is no-cut** — Market-implied odds lock in a hold at 3.50–3.75%. Sticky inflation, a resilient labor market (~4.4% unemployment), and energy-driven PCE upside are keeping Powell's hands tied with no cut expected before late Q3 at the earliest.

💼 **Berkshire acquires homebuilder Taylor Morrison for $6.8B; Fertitta buys Caesars for $17.6B** — Two bellwether deals in one week: Berkshire's homebuilder play signals confidence in housing demand despite mortgage-rate pressure, while Fertitta's Caesars consolidation rewrites the U.S. casino/hospitality map. M&A momentum is real — deal teams are busy and the window is open.

🇪🇺 **EU passes migration overhaul: deportation ramp-up and offshore detention centers** — The European Commission's revamped migration framework clears a major procedural hurdle; critics draw direct comparisons to Trump-era U.S. enforcement. European political risk is rising for multinationals with EU operations — factor this into your regulatory exposure models.

🌍 **Wildcard: 1979 Utah earthquake confirmed at 90km depth — rewriting continental seismology** — Scientists confirmed a seismic event occurred ~90 kilometers underground beneath a continent, far deeper than models predicted was possible. If reproduced, this forces a rethink of North American fault-risk models with real implications for infrastructure siting and insurance underwriting.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

🧠 **Anthropic files confidential S-1, eyes $1.75T IPO valuation in October** — Fresh off a $65B Series H at a $965B post-money valuation and $47B annualized revenue run rate, Anthropic submitted its draft S-1 to the SEC on June 1. An October listing at $1.75–1.8T would be the largest IPO in history. This isn't just a financial event — it resets every enterprise contract negotiation with Anthropic's competitors and signals the AI platform era is entering its monetization phase.

🤖 **Microsoft Build 2026: the PC is now an agentic operating system** — Microsoft open-sourced the Microsoft Agent Framework (MIT license, Python/.NET SDKs) and reframed Windows as a platform where agents plan, remember, and take actions rather than wait for a click. The shift from "click to trigger" to "intent to outcome" is now Microsoft's official developer narrative — enterprise ISVs need to update their roadmap framing accordingly.

💡 **MAI-Code-1-Flash debuts in GitHub Copilot — Microsoft's first in-house coding model** — The 5B-parameter model solves problems with up to 60% fewer tokens than comparable offerings, built on clean-licensed data to reduce OpenAI dependency. Directly relevant to anyone running coding agent pipelines at cost-sensitive scale — benchmark it against Claude Sonnet and GPT-4o-mini before your next contract renewal.

🔌 **Google retires Gemini 2.0 (EOL June 1), pushes 3.x as the new baseline** — The 3.x family ships with improved multi-modal and agentic capabilities, but API surface changes will break naive port-overs. If you're on Gemini 2.x integrations, your migration window is now.

📊 **COMPUTEX 2026: TSMC mulling 15% price hike on 3nm; NVIDIA RTX Spark and AMD Venice 2nm in full ramp** — Jensen Huang and Lisa Su both touched down in Taipei. TSMC's potential 15% surcharge on 3nm (plus another 10% in 2027) is the most consequential cost-structure story in AI infra right now — it hits Nvidia margins directly and will ripple to every hyperscaler's capex model within a quarter.

🔮 **Anthropic's Project Vend turns profitable — agentic AI clears the viability bar** — Claude running a small retail shop end-to-end crossed into profitability. This is the first documented case of a fully autonomous AI-run business turning a profit. The signal isn't the shop — it's that the unit economics of narrow agentic deployment are proving out at small scale before the enterprise deployments hit.

🏛️ **Wildcard: OpenAI ships 6 Codex role plugins with 62 integrated business apps** — Bundles for data analytics, sales, and finance with native connectors to Salesforce, Jira, and ServiceNow. This is OpenAI's clearest move yet toward the enterprise workflow layer, competing directly with Microsoft Copilot Studio and Salesforce Agentforce on their own turf.
