Buffett is making a structural bet on the U.S. housing supply shortage persisting despite rate pressure. It's a contrarian signal worth noting: if the country's best capital allocator is buying homebuilders into a rate scare, the housing supply problem may be more durable than the interest rate problem.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

🧠 **Sam Altman, Demis Hassabis, and Dario Amodei all confirmed for G7 in Évian (June 15–17)** — Macron personally invited the frontier AI lab CEOs; this is the first time AI executives have been formally integrated into a heads-of-state summit agenda. The stated priorities are youth safety and governance frameworks. Whatever communiqué emerges from Évian will shape EU AI Act implementation and the U.S. federal AI policy vacuum simultaneously — read the post-summit language on liability and transparency carefully, as it will travel.

🔮 **SpaceX IPO embeds xAI's AI data center business as a formal growth driver — Musk's compute empire gets a public market price** — The pre-IPO xAI merger means Grok and its associated AI cloud infrastructure are priced into SPCX at $1.78T. This creates direct competitive pressure on Azure, AWS, and Google Cloud from a vertically integrated newcomer with a Starlink edge compute layer already at scale. The cloud wars just gained a well-capitalized, rocket-fueled entrant.

🤖 **AWS expands Amazon Connect into four purpose-built agentic enterprise verticals** — Amazon Connect Decisions (supply chain), Talent (hiring), Customer (engagement), and Health (healthcare admin). Amazon is systematically shifting agentic AI from general-purpose assistant to embedded enterprise workflow layer. The risk for independent agentic platform builders: if your value prop is "we connect AI to enterprise workflows," you now have AWS as a direct competitor with a built-in procurement relationship.

💼 **Microsoft and Google are fighting to catch Claude Code in enterprise AI coding — Gartner flags "new expansion phase"** — Gartner released a report labeling the enterprise AI coding agent market as entering a "new phase of expansion and competitive realignment," with Claude Code as the benchmark. Microsoft Copilot leads on enterprise distribution, Google on model breadth. The differentiator has already shifted from benchmark performance to procurement depth and compliance integration — which is where Microsoft's existing enterprise footprint becomes the decisive variable.

📊 **Enterprise agentic governance is now the primary vendor selection criterion** — Microsoft Purview, Snowflake Horizon, Databricks Unity Catalog, and Google's Agent Governance Framework are in an active feature war over audit trails, data lineage, and compliance dashboards. The signal: enterprise buyers have moved from "can it do the task?" to "can I prove it did the task correctly?" That maturity transition is the leading indicator of real budget allocation — and it's happening now.

💡 **Wildcard: Frontier model pricing has effectively commoditized mid-tier inference** — GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.5 Flash are now at ~$1.50/Mtok input; even Claude Opus 4.8 sits at $5 input/$25 output — dramatically below where the market was 12 months ago. For enterprise builders, this fundamentally changes the build-vs-buy equation: the cost of intelligence is no longer the constraint, the cost of integration and governance is. Reframe your AI economics accordingly.
