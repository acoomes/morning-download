## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

🗳️ **Colombia elects Trump-backed far-right “The Tiger” de la Espriella president** — De la Espriella defeated leftist Iván Cepeda by less than 1% in Sunday’s runoff, ending Petro’s leftist era with the most votes ever cast for a Colombian presidential candidate. Expect sharp pivots on security policy, peace talks with FARC, and a tighter bilateral relationship with Washington — Latin America’s center of gravity just shifted right.

🏦 **Fed holds at 3.5–3.75% but dot plot now points toward hikes, not cuts** — Kevin Warsh’s first FOMC meeting ended with nine of 18 members penciling in at least one hike before year-end; cuts are now pushed to 2027–2028 as Iran-war inflation proves stubbornly sticky. The Fed’s March guidance for cuts has been completely abandoned — the market is now pricing a higher-for-longer regime through the next two years.

📊 **World Bank slashes 2026 global growth forecast to 2.5% — lowest since COVID** — Middle East energy shocks are the culprit; the ZEW sentiment indicator turned positive for the first time since the war began, suggesting fragile green shoots, but the structural drag is significant. Economies dependent on energy imports are getting hit hardest — the divergence between energy-exporting and energy-importing nations is widening fast.

🤝 **Vance in Switzerland for Iran peace talks framework negotiations** — The VP met Iranian representatives in Bern to work the technical framework underpinning last week’s preliminary ceasefire agreement. This is the single biggest macro variable for oil prices, inflation, and geopolitical risk for the rest of 2026 — progress or breakdown here has immediate market consequences.

💼 **Crowe accounting firm sells stake to KKR in ~$3B deal** — Private equity’s appetite for professional services continues; this follows a run of major PE roll-ups in accounting and advisory, signaling the sector is ripe for AI-driven efficiency plays. If your enterprise software touches professional services workflows, this is the deal flow that validates the market.

🕊️ **Wildcard: Pope Leo XIV presented his AI encyclical “Magnifica Humanitas” alongside Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah** — The 42,000-word document is the Vatican’s “Rerum Novarum” for the digital age, explicitly comparing AI’s concentration of power to the Industrial Revolution’s labor abuses. The optics of an Anthropic co-founder at the Vatican are remarkable — and the encyclical is already influencing EU regulatory language on AI governance.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

🏗️ **Trump signs AI Executive Order: voluntary model review + national cybersecurity clearinghouse** — Companies can voluntarily submit frontier models for 30-day pre-release government testing; the EO explicitly prohibits mandatory licensing or pre-clearance. Light-touch by design, but the voluntary review creates a soft norm that could harden — especially relevant for enterprise compliance and government-adjacent AI deployment teams.

🖥️ **NVIDIA RTX Spark Superchip: 1 PFLOP, 128GB unified RAM, runs 120B-parameter LLMs locally** — Unveiled at Computex, debuting in laptops and desktops from Dell, HP, Lenovo, and ASUS this fall. Jensen Huang’s bid to own the local inference layer — a direct shot at Apple Silicon for AI-developer hardware — and could reshape where enterprise AI workloads land as on-prem inference becomes practical.

🧠 **June model wave: Claude Fable 5 broadly available, Gemini 3.5 Pro live, OpenAI drops open-source gpt-oss-120b** — The gap between frontier and open-source is narrowing fast; OpenAI’s 120B model runs on a single GPU and their 20B runs on consumer laptops. The open-weights race is accelerating, which compresses API moats and gives builders new optionality on cost and data privacy.

💰 **StepFun closes $2.5B round — largest Chinese AI funding ever, HK IPO on deck** — Backed by Alibaba, Tencent, and Middle East sovereign wealth funds; valued at ~$10B. China’s “AI Six Tigers” are well-capitalized and advancing toward public markets — watch for export control escalation as Washington’s likely policy response.

🔧 **GitHub Copilot shifts all plans to metered/usage-based billing effective June 1** — Flat-rate unlimited AI use is over; enterprise seats get a credit pool ($39/seat → 3,900 credits), with overages at $0.01/credit. Code completions stay unlimited, but agentic chat, code review, and agent workflows are now metered. Budget discipline for AI developer tooling just became a real CFO conversation.

⚙️ **NVIDIA + TSMC bring AI into fabs: 20–50% cycle-time improvement via cuLitho GPU-accelerated lithography** — TSMC is deploying NVIDIA’s computational lithography platform across its manufacturing lifecycle. This closes the loop on the “AI reinventing every industry” thesis — and it’s measurable: faster wafer cycles could meaningfully improve supply for H-series and B-series GPUs that power the very AI boom driving the demand.

🌐 **Wildcard: MCP release candidate for July 28 drops — largest protocol revision since launch** — The upcoming spec introduces a stateless core for ordinary HTTP infrastructure, making production deployment dramatically simpler. With every major vendor (OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Anthropic) now shipping MCP support, this revision cements the protocol as the universal agentic tool-call layer. **For anyone building agent platforms: read the RC now.**

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** The EU’s “Digital Omnibus” package — quietly consolidating the AI Act, GDPR, the Data Act, and the ePrivacy Directive into a single framework — is advancing with a December 2026 transposition deadline for the revised Product Liability Directive, which now explicitly covers AI software. Enterprise builders shipping AI products into the EU have roughly a 6-month window to model their liability exposure before it becomes binding law — this has almost no press coverage relative to its compliance stakes.

**One-Liner:** The “figure it out later” phase of the AI buildout is ending — from the Fed signaling higher-for-longer to NVIDIA owning every layer of the AI stack to MCP becoming the universal API layer, the cost-and-compliance era has begun.
