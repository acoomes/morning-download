## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

🇮🇷 **US-Iran 60-day ceasefire memorandum holds as markets hit all-time highs** — Oil dropped 10%, Japan's Nikkei surged to historic highs, and every major US index opened at records; peace signals in the Middle East plus Dell's blowout earnings (+30%) gave bulls everything they needed to close out May on a high note. Don't mistake geopolitical relief for structural resolution — 60 days is a pause, not a peace.

📊 **PCE inflation spiked to 3.8% in April — highest since May 2023** — The Fed's preferred gauge is moving the wrong direction: up from 3.5% in March, well above target, and the ECB is signaling its own June hike. Any coordinated global easing narrative is on ice. Builders and CFOs on variable-rate debt should be stress-testing scenarios where rate cuts don't arrive until late 2026 or beyond.

🌏 **Shangri-La Dialogue wraps with Asia openly hedging on the US security umbrella** — Asia's premier defense summit (May 29–31, Singapore) concluded with US Defense Secretary Hegseth warning about China's military buildup while Asian nations — notably absent senior Chinese representation for the second straight year — visibly shopped for alternative security arrangements. For enterprise tech: as defense budgets diversify across Asia-Pacific, procurement and export control dynamics are shifting fast.

💼 **Paramount Skydance's $110.9B acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery awaits regulatory clearance** — Netflix walked away in February after declining to raise its bid; WBD shareholders overwhelmingly approved the Paramount Skydance deal on April 23. Q3 close pending US and international regulatory sign-off. A combined Paramount Skydance-WBD creates the largest studio and streaming content library in the world — and puts enormous pressure on Netflix to keep spending on originals.

📈 **USMCA review deadline is July 1 — 30 days out** — The mandatory joint review of the US-Mexico-Canada Agreement hits in one month. The Trump administration's transactional posture toward both neighbors makes outcomes genuinely uncertain. Supply chains that assumed North American integration as a baseline should be running contingency scenarios now.

🌏 **Wildcard: India commits $500B in US exports over five years** — As part of a broader bilateral trade deal, this is one of the largest economic commitments between the US and any partner in history. Aerospace, energy, tech hardware, and critical minerals are the named beneficiaries — a decade-long demand tailwind for well-positioned players.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

🤖 **Meta Superintelligence Labs launches Muse Spark** — Meta's first model from its newly formed internal AI research division targets multimodal perception, reasoning, health, and agentic workflows. This is Meta's real swing at model-level parity with OpenAI and Anthropic — not an open-weights release, but a frontier model play. Watch benchmark positioning closely over the next few weeks.

📈 **OpenAI confidentially files for IPO targeting September 2026 at $730B valuation** — One of the largest tech IPOs in history would force unprecedented public disclosure on OpenAI's revenue, margins, and Microsoft dependency. The "non-profit to for-profit" structure will get significant scrutiny from institutional investors. Every competitor will be reading that S-1 like a treasure map.

🧠 **Andrej Karpathy joins Anthropic's pre-training team** — Karpathy (ex-OpenAI, Tesla) announced the move May 19 and will lead a new effort using Claude itself to accelerate pre-training research — a meta-loop with serious capability implications. Researchers of his caliber don't move for titles. This move matters more than most model announcements.

🔌 **NVIDIA + TSMC run AI inside the fab itself** — TSMC is deploying NVIDIA H200 GPUs and cuML ML libraries directly in its manufacturing pipeline, yielding 20–50% better lithography cost/cycle time and 50x faster chemistry simulations. This is AI accelerating the hardware that runs AI — a self-reinforcing loop that structurally advantages TSMC and NVIDIA against every other foundry-chipmaker pairing.

🏗️ **Google launches Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform at Cloud Next '26** — Bundles model selection, agent building, DevOps orchestration, and security tooling in one platform — a direct shot at Microsoft's Agent 365, which went GA in May. The enterprise agent platform wars are now a two-horse race. Builders on either platform: your vendor relationships are about to get a lot more strategic.

🛠️ **MCP Dev Summit: 110M SDK downloads/month, 170 enterprise members, Uber running 60K+ agent executions/week** — MCP has crossed from developer experiment to enterprise infrastructure. Forrester is calling 30% of enterprise software vendors shipping native MCP integrations in 2026. If you're building enterprise software and haven't mapped your MCP surface area, you're already behind the wave. **Directly relevant to anyone building agentic platforms.**

💡 **Wildcard: Vapi raises $50M Series B after 1 billion platform calls — Amazon Ring is the proof point** — Voice AI agent infrastructure hitting billion-call scale is a quiet signal that voice-based agentic workflows are moving from demo to production. The detail that matters: Amazon Ring evaluated 40+ AI voice vendors and chose Vapi to route 100% of its inbound calls. That's not a pilot — that's a production bet on AI voice reliability in a high-stakes consumer context.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** When OpenAI's S-1 is made public — expected mid-summer ahead of a September IPO — it will be the most closely studied document in enterprise tech in years. It will finally reveal the real cost structure behind a $730B+ private valuation, the full terms of Microsoft's API exclusivity arrangement, and what the non-profit-to-for-profit conversion actually means for equity holders. Every enterprise AI vendor with a Microsoft or OpenAI dependency should be war-gaming the disclosure scenarios now.

**One-Liner:** Markets are celebrating an Iran ceasefire and AI earnings euphoria while inflation is quietly running hot, trade agreements are remaking global supply chains, and the enterprise agent stack just had its most consequential week of infrastructure maturity yet.
