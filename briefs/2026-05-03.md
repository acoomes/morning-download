# Morning Download — May 3, 2026

## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

- 🛢️ **US-Iran war enters week 10; Trump reviewing 14-point Hormuz deal proposal.** Iran shut the Strait of Hormuz on March 4 — the largest supply disruption in oil market history, taking 14M barrels offline — and Trump says he's now reviewing an Iranian proposal to reopen it. Brent is ~$110/barrel, US gas sits at $4.39/gallon; no deal by mid-May and $5.00 is the floor.

- 🏛️ **Powell holds rates in his final meeting; 8-4 dissent vote is most divided Fed in 34 years.** FOMC kept rates at 3.5–3.75% for the third straight meeting as Powell confirmed he will step down May 15 and remain on the board. Warsh's nomination is advancing through the Senate — whoever chairs the June meeting inherits stagflation risk, an active war premium on energy, and a fractured committee.

- 📈 **S&P 500 and Nasdaq post all-time highs; Meta tanks on $125–145B AI capex plan.** April was the best month for the S&P 500 in five years (+10%), but Meta dropped sharply after guiding to $125–145B in AI capital spending this year despite beating Q1 earnings. Markets are officially drawing a line: AI spend needs a near-term return story, not just a vision.

- ⚖️ **SCOTUS invalidated Trump's IEEPA tariff authority; 10% global tariff now ticking toward a July 24 expiry.** The February ruling forced Trump onto Section 122 of the Trade Act — a 150-day, 10% global tariff that expires July 24. With no trade deals in sight and supply chains already strained by the Iran war, that cliff matters more than it's getting credit for.

- 💰 **AMD lands $60B Meta chip deal; Q1 earnings land May 5.** Meta handed AMD its largest contract in company history — a 5-year, $60B agreement to deploy custom MI450 GPUs across Meta AI data centers starting H2 2026. AMD is up 8% on the news. May 5 earnings are the first real stress test of whether this translates into forward guidance.

- 🤖 **Wildcard: Chinese courts rule companies cannot fire workers specifically to replace them with AI.** Two separate Chinese court rulings (Dec 2025 and this month) have established that AI substitution is not lawful grounds for termination. This is quietly becoming a global labor law precedent that every HR and operations leader at a large enterprise should be tracking.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

- 🏗️ **2026 is Gartner and Forrester's declared breakout year for multi-agent systems — but only 34% of orgs have shipped successfully.** 23% of enterprises are now scaling agentic AI, 62% are experimenting, and 40% of enterprise apps are projected to embed task-specific agents by year-end. The execution gap is real: most teams are losing cycles to data integration debt, governance overhead, and security concerns rather than model capability. *This is where the delivery value is right now.*

- 🧠 **Model stack snapshot: GPT-5.5, Opus 4.7, and Gemini 3.1 Ultra all live.** OpenAI's GPT-5.5 (April 24) pushes hard on agentic coding and computer use. Anthropic's Opus 4.7 is the safer, more literal option — better for regulated enterprise contexts. Google's Gemini 3.1 Ultra ships the largest context window yet. Anthropic's annualized run rate crossed $30B (from $9B at end of 2025) — the model race is now also a revenue race. *Directly relevant for model selection in enterprise AI delivery.*

- 📡 **NVIDIA hits $5T market cap; AI chip spend heading toward $700B in 2026.** Nvidia crossed $5 trillion on April 24, with FY2026 revenue of $215.9B (+65% YoY) and data center at $194B (+68%). BofA raised its 2026 chip sector forecast to $1.3 trillion total. AMD's MI450 deal with Meta is the first credible crack in Nvidia's data center near-monopoly — watch May 5 AMD earnings closely.

- 🔌 **MCP 2026 roadmap targets stateless HTTP transport and horizontal scaling.** The MCP steering committee published its 2026 roadmap: the headline is a stateless HTTP transport variant that removes the requirement for persistent SSE connections, enabling MCP servers to scale horizontally behind standard load balancers. MCP Apps (interactive UI components returned by tools) is now an official extension. *This solves the biggest infrastructure bottleneck for production multi-agent deployments.*

- 🫣 **Microsoft covertly added "Co-Authored-by Copilot" to Git commits — even with AI features disabled.** A VS Code product manager pushed the change through without broad disclosure; it landed in commits for users who had explicitly turned off AI features. The immediate backlash on GitHub and Hacker News was severe and it was reversed. Small incident, but it reveals the trust deficit that embedded AI tooling will keep running into.

- 🍪 **OpenAI turns on marketing cookies by default for free ChatGPT users.** OpenAI is now sharing cookie IDs and email addresses with ad partners (including Instagram) to promote its own products on third-party platforms. This is a monetization tell as much as a privacy story — OpenAI is beginning to behave like an ad-supported consumer platform. Worth watching for downstream enterprise data handling implications.

- 🔬 **Nature paper: human scientists still outperform the best AI agents on complex research tasks.** The Stanford HAI AI Index Report 2026 notes AI mentions in natural sciences publications are at 6–9%, but a concurrent Nature study finds top AI agents still trail humans on genuinely open-ended, complex scientific work. Important calibration data if you're scoping what agent autonomy can actually handle today versus in 12 months.

## Sleeper Story
**The July 24 tariff cliff.** Trump's 10% global fallback tariff (post-SCOTUS) expires in 82 days. With the Iran war straining energy and logistics costs, no major trade deal in sight, and Powell stepping down to be replaced by an unknown-commodity in Warsh, this creates a late-July macro pressure point that almost nobody in the enterprise AI budget conversation is modeling. If it doesn't get resolved or extended quietly, it reprices hardware, cloud infrastructure, and cross-border SaaS contracts right in the middle of Q3 planning cycles.

## One-Liner
*The models keep shipping, the chips keep selling, and the world's oil supply is being held hostage while the Fed's last adult hands the keys to someone new.*
