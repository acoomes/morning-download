## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

🕊️ **Trump threatens Strait of Hormuz tolls as Iran deal enters turbulence** — With the 60-day MOU clock now running, Trump posted to Truth Social threatening to impose “service fees” on ships transiting the strait if a final deal isn’t locked in; JD Vance flew to Switzerland Sunday to accelerate technical talks via Pakistani and Qatari mediators. The agreement that calmed energy markets is looking materially shakier than it did at signing, and crude is watching every post.

🏛️ **Fed’s Warsh drops rate-cut bias in his debut FOMC meeting** — Wednesday’s hold at 3.5–3.75% came with a hawkish gut-punch: nine of 18 Fed officials now expect a rate hike by year-end, PCE inflation was revised up to 3.6%, and all forward-cut language was stripped from the statement. Warsh’s first meeting signals a Fed willing to tighten, not just pause — enterprise CFOs should recheck their floating-rate assumptions heading into H2.

📊 **USMCA July 1 renewal deadline: all three parties expected to miss it** — The US Trade Representative has signaled it won’t recommend renewal without changes; the most likely outcome is a no-renewal that triggers annual review cycles through 2036, manufacturing years of uncertainty for cross-border supply chains in auto, semiconductor assembly, and agricultural trade. Ten days out, no deal in sight.

🏦 **M&A boom accelerates: tech deals hit $282.6B in H1, megadeals up 149% YoY** — Corporate dealmaking is running at its fastest pace since 2021; DOJ clearance of Google’s Wiz acquisition signals a green light for large platform-led security rollups; private equity is competing with strategics at scale again. The deal drought of 2025 is definitively over.

💼 **AMD Venice 2nm server chip enters mass production at TSMC** — The first HPC processor built at the 2nm node is in full ramp at TSMC foundries, making AMD the first to ship at this density at scale; this is a meaningful inflection on the compute density curve and a direct competitive pressure on Intel’s struggling process roadmap.

🌍 **Wildcard: MIT demonstrates single-fuel system for both chemical and electric spacecraft thrusters** — Researchers showed one propellant can power both quick-burst chemical engines and long-range ion drives, eliminating the weight penalty of dual fuel systems; compact deep-space missions just got materially more viable, and the implications for commercial satellite economics are significant.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

🧠 **Fable 5/Mythos 5 still offline — Trump met Amodei at G7, lift expected “soon”** — Nine days after the government-ordered suspension, Anthropic’s most capable models remain dark; the White House confirmed Trump eased concerns after meeting Dario Amodei at the G7 in Évian, and David Sacks said the restriction lifts the moment a specific jailbreak is patched. Every day Fable 5 sits offline is a compounding market-share gift to OpenAI and Google, whose models are fully available.

🔮 **Great American AI Act moves in Congress with bipartisan support** — The 269-page Obernolte-Trahan draft (introduced June 4) would create a new federal AI agency (FAIGO inside Commerce), mandate third-party safety audits for frontier models, require critical safety incident reporting, and institute a 3-year preemption of state AI laws. That preemption clause alone is the most significant US regulatory action for enterprise AI since the EU AI Act — it kills the 50-state compliance nightmare in one stroke.

🤖 **JetBrains Junie exits beta with GA release, agentic debugging, and ACP protocol** — As of June 17, JetBrains’ coding agent is generally available across IntelliJ, PyCharm, and WebStorm, plus a standalone CLI; the GA build runs on ACP (Agent Communication Protocol), supports MCP server connections, and uses the IDE’s actual debugger and test runner rather than a simulated environment. JetBrains Air — a multi-agent environment running Codex, Claude, Gemini, and Junie simultaneously — is now shipping alongside it.

📊 **NVIDIA and TSMC deploy AI across the full chip fab lifecycle** — The two companies announced TSMC is applying NVIDIA accelerated computing across semiconductor design, manufacturing, yield analysis, and fab operations; this isn’t a product launch, it’s a manufacturing infrastructure play that compounds TSMC’s already-dominant yield advantage and makes it harder for Intel and Samsung to close the gap.

🔌 **MCP crosses 97M monthly SDK downloads; A2A v1.2 in production at 150+ enterprises** — Anthropic’s Model Context Protocol, now under the Linux Foundation’s Agentic AI Foundation, is the de facto standard for connecting agents to external tools; Google’s A2A protocol reached v1.2 and is running in production at Microsoft, AWS, Salesforce, SAP, and ServiceNow among others. The protocol layer underneath agentic systems has effectively settled.

🛠️ **GitHub Copilot transitions to usage-based metered billing with GitHub AI Credits** — GitHub’s June 1 shift from request-based to metered billing is now hitting enterprise billing cycles; light users will see lower costs but heavy agentic use gets more expensive. Expect procurement teams to start auditing Copilot usage aggressively as the first metered invoices land.

💡 **Wildcard: Only 11% of organizations running agents in production despite 79% claiming adoption** — The O’Reilly AI Agents Stack 2026 report documents the yawning gap between executive checkbox-ticking and actual deployment; the real structural signal is that 66.4% of the agentic market now targets coordinated multi-agent systems rather than single agents — the engineering problem has shifted from “can we build an agent?” to “can we coordinate 50 of them reliably?”

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** The USMCA July 1 deadline getting quietly missed will ripple through North American manufacturing supply chains for years — particularly auto and semiconductor assembly — but it’s buried under Iran ceasefire coverage. The cascading effects of annual review uncertainty will hit cross-border industrial planning this fall, well before the media catches up.

**One-Liner:** The week’s theme is “signed but not settled” — the Iran deal has a 60-day clock and a threatening post, Fable 5 has a government patch-list, and USMCA has a deadline nobody’s meeting.
