## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

- 🌊 **Japan 7.4 earthquake elevates megaquake risk** — A magnitude 7.4 struck off Japan's northeast coast Monday, triggering tsunami warnings (later lifted); the government warns of heightened megaquake probability for the next week. Matters because a major Japan disruption means supply chain shock for TSMC-adjacent semiconductor assembly and global shipping — the last thing an already-stressed market needs.

- 🛢️ **US-Iran ceasefire expiring; Strait of Hormuz still closed** — Tehran is sending a delegation to Islamabad for a second round of peace talks, but the Hormuz strait remains closed, driving U.S. gas to $4.05/gallon (a 2022-era pain point). Futures are cautiously up on talk-optimism (Dow +0.6%, Nasdaq +0.4%), but the macro backdrop is ugly: Consumer Sentiment just hit its all-time historic low, and March CPI hit a 2-year high.

- 🍎 **Apple names John Ternus as next CEO, effective Sept. 1** — Tim Cook's 14-year run ends; Ternus is a hardware/engineering insider. Markets are watching whether Apple doubles down on its AI Services pivot or returns to hardware-first discipline. This is one of the most consequential CEO transitions in tech in a decade.

- 🏛️ **EU revises Technology Transfer Block Exemption Regulation, effective May 1** — The European Commission's updated TTBER clarifies AI and data licensing, joint negotiation structures, and competition rules for tech. This lands in 10 days and will quietly reshape how software and AI companies structure deals in Europe. Not enough people are paying attention.

- 💊 **Abbott acquires Exact Sciences for ~$21B** — Major medtech consolidation: Abbott expands its clinical diagnostics portfolio with Exact Sciences' cancer screening franchise (Cologuard). Signals the diagnostics space is heating up as AI-driven early detection becomes a real commercial category.

- 🌶️ **Wildcard: McCormick acquires a Unilever food business for $45 billion** — That is not a rounding error. One of the largest consumer goods deals in history, slipping beneath the Iran/Apple headlines. If it closes, McCormick becomes a very different company.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

- 🔒 **Anthropic's Claude Mythos exists — and you can't have it** — Anthropic confirmed Claude Mythos is its most capable model ever (83.1% on CyberGym vs. 66.6% for the previous best) but it's locked behind a 50-company firewall with no public release. Separately, OpenAI released a cybersecurity-focused model on April 14. Both labs shipped restricted cyber-AI models within 7 days of each other — that convergence is not a coincidence, and it signals frontier labs are sitting on capabilities they don't trust themselves to release broadly.

- 🤖 **Meta launches Muse Spark** — First release from Meta Superintelligence Labs' new Muse series, the first major model since Meta's $14.3B acquisition of Scale AI and Alexandr Wang's team. Meta is still playing benchmark catch-up to Google and OpenAI, but the talent and capital are now in place. Watch the next 2-3 releases.

- 🏗️ **Salesforce Headless 360: every platform capability becomes an MCP tool** — Announced at TDX, this exposes all Salesforce capabilities as APIs, MCP tools, and CLI commands so AI agents can operate Salesforce without a browser. Directly relevant to agentic platform builders — this is a significant free expansion of the MCP tool surface for enterprise workflows.

- 🛡️ **Databricks Unity AI Gateway: governance finally arrives for agentic AI** — AI Gateway is now part of Unity Catalog, extending the same permissions, auditing, and policy controls to how agents access LLMs, MCP servers, and APIs. Enterprise agent adoption stalls on governance; this removes one of the biggest blockers.

- 💾 **TSMC Q1: revenue +35% YoY, net income +58% — AI compute spend is accelerating** — TSMC's blowout quarter (guided >30% full-year 2026 growth) confirms the AI infrastructure buildout is not plateauing. NVIDIA has reserved the majority of TSMC's advanced CoWoS packaging capacity; AMD posted Data Center revenue up 39% YoY to $5.38B. The compute arms race is intact despite macro headwinds.

- ☁️ **Cloudflare wraps "Agents Week": new primitives for the agentic cloud** — Cloudflare shipped a wave of capabilities for building and deploying AI agents at the edge. Worth reviewing if you're architecting distributed agentic systems — edge-native execution sidesteps latency and cost issues in centralized orchestration.

- 📊 **Wildcard: PwC finds 75% of AI's economic gains captured by just 20% of companies** — PwC's 2026 AI Performance Study shows leaders are focused on *growth*, not just cost reduction, and the gap between AI leaders and laggards is widening fast. The window for catching up is closing.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** The EU's revised Technology Transfer Block Exemption Regulation (TTBER) takes effect **May 1** — 10 days from now. It reshapes how AI and software companies structure data licensing and IP deals in Europe, including AI model licensing and joint negotiation between platforms. Nobody in the AI industry is talking about it. Enterprise legal and product teams should be briefed this week.

**One-Liner:** *The world is simultaneously closing the Strait of Hormuz and building AI models too dangerous to release publicly — welcome to Q2 2026, where every headline is either a supply shock or a capability leak.*
