## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

🪖 **US-Iran war enters third week of nightly strikes — ceasefire is officially dead** — CENTCOM ran a third consecutive night of strikes on Iranian infrastructure in Hormozgan province (bridges, a railway station feeding Bandar Abbas port), while Iran retaliated by disabling two supertankers in Omani territorial waters, killing an Indian crew member. The Strait of Hormuz closure is now official Tehran policy. Brent crude pushed above $87/bbl and any peace premium in equity markets has been fully priced out — the S&P is now 1% off its 52-week high.

📊 **Fed's Jefferson opens door to a rate hike — July 28-29 FOMC suddenly matters** — The S&P 500 slipped 0.51% to 7,534 as Fed Vice Chair Jefferson said he'd support a rate hike later this year if inflation doesn't cooperate. The June dot plot already shifted median year-end rate projections up from 3.4% to 3.8%; Chairman Warsh is calling the internal debate a "family fight." IMF's July update confirmed disinflation has stalled globally. War-driven energy costs + resilient labor markets = central banks are stuck, and anyone pricing in cuts this year is now offsides.

🚀 **Rocket Lab acquires Iridium for $8B — direct SpaceX challenger takes shape** — Announced June 29 and closing through mid-2027, this deal unites Rocket Lab's launch and satellite manufacturing with Iridium's global spectrum network. The combined entity becomes the first end-to-end vertically integrated space company outside SpaceX. For enterprise connectivity and defense buyers, competition in LEO just got a lot more real.

🇬🇧 **Andy Burnham formally named UK Labour leader, entering Downing Street Monday** — The former Greater Manchester mayor will be the UK's next Prime Minister, officially taking office July 20. He arrives inheriting an active conflict in the Middle East and a stagflationary macro environment. Watch his posture on UK-US tech and trade relations closely — Burnham is considerably more skeptical of Silicon Valley than his predecessor.

💰 **Adyen acquires AI billing startup Orb for $335M** — The Dutch payments giant is buying billing-software provider Orb to add AI-startup usage-based billing capabilities to its platform. A niche deal with outsized signal: the plumbing for consumption-based AI pricing is becoming a genuine M&A target category as every software company reprices around tokens and compute.

🌍 **Wildcard: Nikkei crashes 4.5% as Asian AI trade unwinds** — Japanese tech stocks led a sharp Asian selloff as the AI-driven rally showed signs of mean reversion. Whether this is a pause or a rotation will become clearer at the July 28 FOMC, but anyone who's been long Nikkei tech as an AI proxy had a bad Thursday.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

🧠 **Google Gemini 3.5 Pro expected today — specs unconfirmed, launch not yet official** — July 17 has been the leaked target date after a slip from June, but as of this writing Google has posted no model card, no pricing page, and no public API listing. Leaked specs cite a 2M-token context window, Deep Think extended reasoning, and input pricing around $1.25/M tokens — but every number is third-party. If it does ship today it would be the largest context window at the frontier by 2x and meaningfully cheaper than GPT-5.6 Sol on input cost. Watch Google's developer blog; until an official post appears, treat this as imminent rather than confirmed.

🌐 **Xi attends 2026 World AI Conference in Shanghai in person for the first time** — Shanghai's World Artificial Intelligence Conference opens today with President Xi attending personally, the first time since the event began in 2018. Beijing is now treating AI leadership as a top-tier national-security priority alongside ByteDance's Seedream model and growing Wall Street adoption of Chinese frontier models. The two-superpower AI race is no longer a forecast — it's the present reality.

🤖 **Grok Build open-sourced — plan mode, 8 parallel subagents, 2M context, MCP support** — xAI released Grok Build as open source with multi-agent orchestration baked in: up to 8 concurrent subagents, 2M-token context, and native MCP support. This is the most capable free, open-source agentic scaffold available today. For teams evaluating orchestration frameworks, Grok Build just moved from "interesting" to "worth a serious prototype."

🔌 **MCP: 16 months old, now embedded in every major coding agent — discovery is the new bottleneck** — Model Context Protocol has gone from emerging standard to table stakes faster than almost any developer protocol in recent memory. Every major coding agent — Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Copilot, Windsurf, Kiro, Grok Build — now speaks MCP. The protocol problem is solved; the discovery problem (finding and vetting quality MCP servers for your stack) is the new friction. Expect a wave of MCP registries and curated server catalogs in H2 2026.

📊 **TSMC Q2 sales surge 36%, warns of 5-10% wafer price hikes for Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm** — NVIDIA's 85% YoY revenue growth and AMD's 57% Data Center growth confirm demand is intact. But TSMC just flagged price increases to its largest customers. This is the moment the AI compute cost curve inflects upward — everyone from hyperscalers to AI startups should expect margin compression on compute within the next contract cycle.

💡 **200+ economists and 16 Nobel laureates: "Act now on AI's economic impact"** — An open letter signed by over 200 economists and AI researchers demands policymakers prepare immediately for AI's labor-market disruption. The signal here isn't the letter — it's the credentialing: 16 Nobel laureates co-signing means this argument has cleared the mainstream-economics establishment. Regulatory and labor-market intervention timelines just accelerated.

🔮 **Wildcard: Claude Cowork's offline persistent task delegation is a different paradigm** — Anthropic's Claude Cowork (launched July 7) handles email, calendar, and file tasks that keep running even when the device is offline. This is not chat with memory — it's task-state that persists across sessions and device sleep. For enterprise workflow automation, the architectural implication is significant: AI that operates asynchronously in the background, not synchronously on demand, changes how you design approval flows, audit trails, and handoff points.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** TSMC's wafer price hike warning (5-10% to Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm) is getting almost no attention next to the Gemini and WAIC headlines. But every AI compute stack downstream of TSMC is about to get more expensive — this will compress margins for hyperscalers, AI startups, and enterprise AI budgets when contracts renew in Q3/Q4. Model inference costs were falling; wafer costs are now going the other direction.

**One-Liner:** Google may or may not have doubled the industry's context window today, Xi showed up at an AI conference for the first time in years, and Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz again — the vibe is "everything is accelerating, including the parts that scare you."
