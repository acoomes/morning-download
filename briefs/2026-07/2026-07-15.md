## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

🏭 **TSMC June revenue up 68% YoY — N3 chips sold out through year-end, GPU lead times hit 36–52 weeks** — TSMC posted all-time June revenue records with Q2 at $39.6B; N3 nodes are fully committed through December. The global semiconductor market is now projected at $1.3T in 2026, up from $1.0T forecast just months earlier. Hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) are collectively spending ~$650B on AI infrastructure this year — an 80% YoY increase. Advanced CoWoS packaging remains the binding supply constraint. TSMC reports Thursday; this call is a live proxy read on the entire AI hardware ecosystem for H2.

🔌 **MCP 2.0 final spec ships July 28 — RC has been live since May 21, migration window closes in two weeks** — The largest revision since MCP launched: the session layer is removed entirely (no more Mcp-Session-Id), making any MCP request routable to any server instance. The RC published May 21 opened a ten-week validation window; the final spec locks July 28. Servers that previously required sticky sessions, shared state stores, and deep packet inspection at the gateway can now run behind a plain round-robin load balancer. Also included: MCP Apps (server-rendered UIs), Tasks extension for long-running work, and formal OAuth/OIDC alignment. **For agentic platform builders:** the RC is available now — if your production architecture still depends on session state, the clock is running.

🤖 **Microsoft Foundry Agent Service hits GA — opinionated enterprise multi-agent stack now in production** — Early July GA ships sandboxed sessions, a Toolboxes system for managed skills and tools, three persistent memory types, Voice Live for real-time voice agents, and built-in multi-agent orchestration patterns. Microsoft also has 60+ enterprise MCP servers ready for the catalog. This is the layer that turns scattered AI experiments into governed, auditable production deployments — the enterprise IT department's answer to "how do we run agents at scale."

💰 **Anthropic in early talks with Samsung to build custom Claude inference chips** — Facing a reported $1.25B/month compute bill, Anthropic is exploring dedicated inference silicon with Samsung to attack unit economics directly. This mirrors the Google TPU playbook: when GPU costs are eating your margin, you design your way out. Success would transform Anthropic's cost structure; failure would validate NVIDIA's moat. Either outcome is strategically important for the competitive landscape.

🧠 **ICML 2026 wrapped in Seoul last week with record 23,918 submissions — agentic AI safety dominated the workshop agenda** — "Agents in the Wild" and "Statistical Frameworks for Uncertainty in Agentic Systems" headlined the July 10–11 workshops. The academic community's research priorities have now caught up with the product agenda: safety, uncertainty quantification, and governance for autonomous agents are first-class research problems, not appendices. Expect the next 12 months of enterprise AI safety tooling to trace directly back to the frameworks presented here.

📊 **79% of enterprises claim agent deployments — only 11% are in production (IBM Think 2026 data)** — The adoption gap deserves repeating: the overwhelming majority of enterprise "AI agent" programs are pilots, slide decks, or proofs-of-concept with dedicated Slack channels. Vendors slow-walking interoperability while figuring out monetization is a large part of why. The 11% who've cleared this hurdle are accruing serious competitive distance from the field.

🔮 **Wildcard: A single gut bacterium eliminated colorectal tumors in mice with one treatment** — Researchers identified a bacterium derived from amphibian intestines that completely eliminated colorectal tumors in mice in a single-dose treatment. This is very early-stage science, but the mechanism — live bacteria as a precision oncology delivery system — is novel enough that it's already drawing serious attention from oncology labs. If it replicates in primate models, this is a major story within 18–24 months.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** The MCP 2.0 RC has been live since May 21 — but most teams haven't touched it yet, and the final spec locks July 28. The removal of session state from the protocol layer means every production MCP deployment built on sticky sessions needs to be re-architected before the final lands. Teams that internalize the new stateless model this week will have a meaningful head start; teams that wait for July 28 will be in reactive mode when enterprise customers start asking why their MCP servers still require sticky routing.
