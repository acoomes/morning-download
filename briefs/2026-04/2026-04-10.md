## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

- 🕊️ **Iran ceasefire holding — barely.** Only 9 ships have transited the Strait of Hormuz since the April 8 ceasefire vs. 100+ pre-war; JD Vance flies to Islamabad Saturday for permanent-deal negotiations. The energy-inflation relief rally is real, but "nine tankers" is not a functioning Strait — markets are pricing in a resolution that hasn't happened yet.

- 🚀 **Artemis II splashes down today off San Diego.** Commander Reid Wiseman, pilot Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen return after setting the farthest-human-from-Earth record at 252,757 miles — first crewed lunar mission since Apollo 17 in 1972, and the first to include a woman, person of color, and non-US citizen beyond LEO. Buried under Iran headlines, but this is the kind of story people will remember when everything else fades.

- ⚖️ **Tariff regime restructured after IEEPA ruling; 60+ new Section 301 investigations launched.** The February Supreme Court ruling stripped IEEPA tariff authority; the administration pivoted to a 10% ad valorem Section 122 surcharge (valid up to 150 days). Separately, USTR opened Section 301 investigations into forced labor and industrial overcapacity targeting China, the EU, and 14 other economies. The largest US tax increase as a percent of GDP since 1993 is now on shakier legal footing — and a 60-investigation pipeline means years of compliance uncertainty for global supply chains.

- 📈 **TSMC Q1 revenue +35% YoY to $35.6B — a record; CoWoS packaging emerges as next bottleneck.** Nvidia now surpasses Apple as TSMC's largest customer. Nvidia has reserved the majority of TSMC's CoWoS advanced packaging capacity; TSMC is already outsourcing steps to third parties and building four new packaging facilities. The chip supply constraint is shifting from silicon to packaging — plan your infrastructure roadmaps accordingly.

- 💹 **S&P 500 closes at 6,824 (+0.62%); Q1 earnings season kicks off with 13.2% YoY EPS growth expected.** Tech leads at 18–22% growth, driven by AI cloud and semiconductor revenue. Markets are pricing in AI monetization delivering — but the Fed is holding at 3.5–3.75% with 2026 PCE forecast at 2.7%, keeping rate-cut hopes modest and PE deal math constrained.

- 🐄 **Wildcard: Veronika the cow documented using both ends of a stick as tools.** First confirmed multi-purpose tool use by a bovine, published in peer-reviewed literature this week. Veronika has a better tool-use story than most enterprise software demos, and I will not elaborate further.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

- 🔒 **Anthropic's Claude Mythos won't ship publicly — too dangerous.** The leaked 10T-parameter model (March 26 leak, April 7 preview) can autonomously find high-severity zero-day vulnerabilities in every major OS and browser. Anthropic is restricting it to 12 Project Glasswing partners (Amazon, Apple, Microsoft, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Linux Foundation, Broadcom, others) for defensive security work only. This is the first frontier model explicitly withheld on safety grounds — the capability bar has moved past what Anthropic will release. If you're building enterprise security tooling, Glasswing partner access is now a competitive moat.

- 🟢 **Google Gemma 4 drops: open-weight, Apache 2.0, frontier-level benchmarks.** Now the leading open-weight model for inference, fine-tuning, and agentic deployments. Apache 2.0 licensing removes the commercialization friction that limited earlier open models — this directly undercuts API-first deployment economics for use cases where latency and cost matter more than absolute capability ceiling.

- 🔌 **MCP v2.1 ships + Microsoft Agent Framework 1.0 GA.** v2.1 adds Server Cards for structured capability discovery via .well-known URLs; 97M monthly SDK downloads; all major platforms (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, AWS, Cloudflare) now ship MCP-native. Agent Framework 1.0 launches with full MCP + A2A 1.0 support for cross-framework agent collaboration. MCP is the USB-C layer — build MCP-native or build tech debt. A2A interoperability means multi-vendor agent pipelines are now an engineering problem, not a research one.

- ⚡ **xAI Colossus 2 expands to 1.5 GW; Grok 4.20 Beta 2 live with four-agent architecture.** Grok (coordinator) + Harper (research) + Benjamin (logic/math) + Lucas (contrarian analysis) run in parallel with cross-verified outputs. xAI's vertical integration of AI compute into SpaceX energy and infrastructure is a bundling play hyperscalers structurally can't replicate.

- 🏭 **TSMC CoWoS packaging becomes the AI compute choke point.** Nvidia has locked up majority capacity; TSMC is outsourcing overflow and building 4 new facilities with 2+ year lead times. If you're planning AI infrastructure for 2027–2028, packaging availability needs to be in your capacity model, not just GPU die counts.

- 🤝 **Snowflake + OpenAI $200M strategic partnership targets enterprise agentic AI at scale.** Data platform + frontier model bundling is the recurring enterprise AI go-to-market pattern. Snowflake is betting the data layer wins over pure LLM API access — watch for similar moves from Databricks and Palantir.

- 🪖 **Wildcard: Defense startup debuts purpose-built agentic AI for battlefield decision support.** Multi-agent architecture adapted from commercial agentic patterns, hardened for military applications. Defense AI procurement is moving fast and mostly under the radar — the same orchestration patterns being debated in enterprise software are being operationalized in high-stakes environments with very different evaluation criteria.
