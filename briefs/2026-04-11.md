# Morning Download — April 11, 2026

## Section 1: World & Market Briefing

- **Iran-US ceasefire is holding — barely — while Islamabad talks begin.** VP JD Vance and Iranian FM Araghchi are face-to-face in Pakistan, but Iran is still charging $1M+ per-ship tolls through the Strait of Hormuz, calling it a ceasefire violation response. Oil is below $100/bbl but stuck there; the market is treating this as a pause, not a resolution. If talks collapse in Islamabad, energy inflation and global trade disruption snap right back.

- **Artemis II crew safely splashes down in the Pacific — first crewed lunar flyby in 53 years.** Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen returned April 10 after a 10-day mission around the Moon. Completely buried under Iran/tariff news, but this is the mission that validates Artemis architecture before landing crews. Worth noting while everyone is staring at the Middle East.

- **Supreme Court-killed IEEPA tariffs replaced with Section 232/301 tariffs; pharma imports now face up to 100% duties.** Average effective tariff rate sits at 13.7% — the largest US tax increase as share of GDP since 1993. Fed's Waller says inflation impact may be transitory; Powell is less sure and acknowledged stagflation risk. Section 301 comment period closes April 15 — this directly affects enterprise software supply chains relying on offshore hardware.

- **SpaceX acquires xAI at $1.25T — the largest tech acquisition ever recorded.** Q1 2026 cross-border M&A hit $454.7B (up 47% YoY). Also in play: AMD-Meta's $60B Instinct MI450 GPU deal (more below), McCormick absorbing Unilever's food portfolio ($65B), and Zurich snapping up Lloyd's insurer Beazley. The deal machine is running at full sprint — AI assets commanding multiples that make everything else look cheap.

- **AI regulation: 600+ state bills in 2026 sessions, federal policy gridlocked, DOJ challenging state AI laws.** Colorado AI Act takes effect June 30. Indiana, Utah, Washington, Oregon, and Idaho passed laws restricting AI in healthcare and chatbots. DOJ's new AI Litigation Task Force is actively contesting state statutes as unconstitutional. Enterprise software companies with US exposure now have to track 50-state compliance variance — this is a material operational risk.

- **Wildcard: Peru heads to the polls with 35 candidates and no front-runner.** Would be the country's 9th president in less than a decade amid surging corruption and organized crime. A cautionary tale about what democratic fragility looks like at scale — and a reminder that political instability in Latin America tends to eventually move commodity markets.

## Section 2: AI & Agentic Systems Briefing

- **Anthropic's Claude Mythos: found thousands of zero-days (including a 27-year-old OpenBSD bug), won't ship publicly.** Gated under Project Glasswing with 12 partners: Amazon, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Linux Foundation, Microsoft, and Palo Alto Networks. Fed and Treasury convened Wall Street CEOs on the systemic cybersecurity risk. This is Anthropic confirming a "step change" in capabilities they're scared to release. If this is what defensive use looks like, think hard about what offensive use implies.

- **OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google formally united against Chinese model distillation theft via Frontier Model Forum.** April 6–7 announcement; labs are now sharing adversarial distillation intelligence to protect frontier model IP. China's AI labs have been training against outputs from US frontier models at scale. This is a competitive moat defense move — and it signals how seriously the labs view capability leakage as a strategic threat.

- **MCP hits v2.1 with Server Cards, 97M monthly SDK downloads, 10K+ public servers.** AWS's Clare Liguori and Anthropic's Den Delimarsky join as maintainers. Server Cards expose structured metadata via `.well-known` URLs, enabling agent-to-server discovery without connecting. This is MCP becoming infrastructure-grade — if you're building agentic platforms, the Server Cards standard changes how you architect tool discovery. Direct relevance.

- **Gartner: 40% of enterprise apps will embed task-specific AI agents by end of 2026 — up from <5% in 2025.** Global AI closed a production deployment with a Fortune 500 pharma company across regulatory reporting and payroll. Enterprise AI ROI averaging 171% vs. traditional automation. The "pilot purgatory" era is ending — procurement is moving.

- **AMD lands the largest GPU procurement deal in history: $60B, multi-year with Meta for Instinct MI450 GPUs.** NVIDIA still dominates (87% market share, $215.9B FY2026 revenue vs. AMD's $34.6B), but AMD is winning where the total addressable market is expanding fastest. Meta's diversification away from pure NVIDIA dependency is the signal — other hyperscalers will follow.

- **TSMC: Nvidia locks in majority of advanced CoWoS packaging capacity; GigaFab Arizona expansion planned; 3nm sold out through 2028.** NVIDIA Vera Rubin (Vera CPU + Rubin GPU, 3nm) enters production H2 2026 targeting 10x inference cost reduction vs. Blackwell. If you're planning GPU infrastructure beyond 2026, supply chain is the binding constraint, not architecture or budget.

- **Wildcard: UC San Diego publishes chip design rethinking GPU power conversion for dramatic data center efficiency gains.** As power constraints become the bottleneck for AI infrastructure (not just supply), this kind of research pipeline matters. Watch for hyperscaler licensing or acquisition interest within 12–18 months.

## Closing

**Sleeper Story:** The MCP maintainer team expansion — specifically Clare Liguori from AWS — signals that MCP governance is formalizing with hyperscaler buy-in. The Server Cards standard could make MCP server discovery as automatic as DNS lookup. Any enterprise agentic platform that hasn't baked in MCP-native tool discovery is accumulating architectural debt that will surface within the next 30 days as the ecosystem races ahead.

**One-Liner:** The Iran ceasefire is leaking, Artemis came home quietly, and AI's biggest story this week isn't a new model launch — it's a Pandora's box Anthropic built, showed to Apple and Microsoft, and then locked back up.
